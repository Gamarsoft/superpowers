1. Diagnose the missing experience-design work

Inspect the actual candidate instructions, pilot decisions and rendered screens. Distinguish missing guidance from guidance that existed but was poorly applied or evaluated. Do not assume adding another checklist will fix the problem.

Frontend-direction should translate the approved product requirements into an experience brief, with focused discussion where decisions remain unresolved:
- Who uses this screen, how often, and with what familiarity?
- What are they trying to accomplish, and what must they understand or decide?
- What should be immediately visible, secondary, contextual or progressively disclosed?
- What feedback lets them judge their changes and act confidently?
- What interaction model, density, vocabulary and visual character suit this product?

Reuse established business context. Ask only questions that materially affect the experience. Bring concrete recommendations and tradeoffs rather than making me invent the design.

2. Strengthen design exploration and selection

For substantial design work with unresolved composition choices, compare a small number of meaningfully different approaches before polishing one. Use inexpensive sketches or rough Paper layouts. Variants must differ in task flow, information hierarchy or interaction structure, not merely colors and card styling.

Choose based on ease of use, clarity, visual quality and fit with the product. Avoid making multiple alternatives mandatory for routine changes or already-settled patterns.

Preserve business behavior and legitimate technical constraints, but distinguish those from inherited presentation choices. 4. Make design quality consequential

A design should be able to fail review despite valid packets, complete exports and functioning components.

Evaluate the rendered proposal for:
- Clear hierarchy and an obvious next action.
- Appropriate interaction patterns and manageable cognitive load.
- Useful visual feedback rather than duplicated field values.
- Concise, user-facing language without implementation terminology.
- Deliberate typography, spacing, alignment and grouping.
- Responsive task organization rather than indiscriminate stacking.
- Appropriate empty, editing, error and completion states.

Ground critiques in visible problems and their consequences. Avoid ceremonial principle citations, arbitrary scores or claims of usability testing that did not happen. Integrate this into the existing review cadence rather than adding an unbounded chain of reviewers.
