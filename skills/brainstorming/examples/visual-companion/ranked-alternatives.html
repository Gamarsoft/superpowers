Rank three release-note entry points

Judged for repeat discoverability inside the analytics product after launch-day hype fades, not for one-time announcement reach alone.

Current winner: Option A is the Recommended starting point this sprint because it stays visible in the reporting workflow without forcing an interruption. Options B and C remain worth seeing because they trade reach against calmness and implementation effort differently.

1

Option A · Inline release banner

A slim banner sits directly under the dashboard date filters with a one-line summary, a “What changed in forecasting” link, and a dismiss action that remembers the current release version.

2

Option B · Activity feed card

A richer card appears in the existing right-rail activity feed with thumbnail, headline, and two bullets about what changed in the release.

Lower interruption, weaker default visibility
Activity feed

Why it ranks second: it is calmer than a banner and almost free to ship, but the feed is often collapsed on narrower laptops and many users skip the rail entirely when they are heads-down.

3

Option C · Modal on first login

A centered modal takes over the screen once per release with three bullets, a hero illustration, and a primary CTA to open the full changelog.

Highest attention spike, weakest repeat discoverability

Why it ranks third: it guarantees attention once, but it is the most interruptive treatment, weak for repeat discovery, and easy for users to dismiss before they understand what changed.
